===========================================================================
WHY THE SCRIPT IS THE SHAPE IT IS
===========================================================================

It opens by naming the declines. Pretending they did not happen is the one thing that would
make this read as another approach from someone not paying attention. Naming them, once,
lightly, and then not mentioning them again, is what earns the next forty seconds.

"So here's the thing itself instead" is the entire pitch. He declined a conversation about a
platform. He is not being asked to have that conversation again; he is being shown the thing
the conversation was about.

The middle scene is the leaderboard rather than the campaign mechanics, because his problem
is not incentive design. It is that his retailers sell several brands and XPENG is the
unfamiliar one. A screen that ranks named people on what they did is the one that answers
"will my network actually engage with this".

"Two more orders. Not a pep talk, a number." is the only line in the script with any edge on
it. It earns its place because every sales director has sat through the pep talk version.

No figure is read aloud. The pages say the data is illustrative.

The CTA routes back through Melissa on purpose. He has already ignored a direct approach from
Phil, so a second one is the same mistake twice. Going back through the person who raised it
internally makes saying yes a smaller thing than saying yes to a stranger.

The video never mentions IM Group, never names Melissa on screen, never says AI, never shows
a price, and never opens the commercial pages.


===========================================================================
DELIVERY NOTES
===========================================================================

Record the three scenes separately and cut them together. One clean audio take laid over the
assembled picture.

British, unhurried, slightly flat. The reference is a man turning his laptop round.

"You turned down a call twice, which was fair enough" has to land as genuinely unbothered. If
it sounds pointed, the video is dead in the first five seconds. Say it the way you would say
it to someone you like.

The one silence, over the retailer list, is worth protecting. Fourteen retailers moving past
is what makes the network look real rather than mocked up.

Do not add music.

The last line is delivered over Dan Whitfield's record still on screen. Do not cut to black,
a logo, or a card with contact details on it.
