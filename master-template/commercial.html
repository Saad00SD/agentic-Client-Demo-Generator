Why it is built this way

Adopting a network programme is usually assumed to mean an enterprise-wide rollout, and that assumption is what turns a good first meeting into "let me think about it". A pilot answers it cheaply: one market or one objective, measured against the baseline that existed before it started.

Each stage earns the next on evidence rather than on faith. If the pilot does not move the numbers, nothing scales, and that is the point.

What this page deliberately does not do

It does not quote. A programme spanning a whole network prices differently from a single-market pilot, and anchoring the first to the second helps nobody. Scope, network size, participant numbers, integrations, languages and reward funding all move the number.

What it does is set out the components, so that when the conversation reaches money both sides already agree on what is being bought.
